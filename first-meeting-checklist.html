A calmer first meetup starts with a clearer plan

Meeting someone new should not require guessing about safety, transport, money or boundaries. Use this practical checklist for a first strictly platonic coffee, outing or online conversation.

By Sathivo · Updated 14 October 2026 · For adults 18+ · Strictly platonic

1. Before you agree: choose the kind of plan

Start small. For a first in-person arrangement, a short coffee conversation or a walk in a busy, familiar public area is easier to manage than a long trip or a private event. If you prefer online conversation, agree on the format, start time and expected length rather than sharing personal details immediately.

Sathivo is for adults aged 18 and above and strictly platonic interactions. No booking should be treated as consent to romance, touch, sexual activity, moving to a private location, or anything beyond the agreed activity. Both people can decline a request.

2. Agree on a public and practical meeting point

Pick a café, mall common area, public exhibition, park entrance during staffed or busy hours, or another place you know is open and easy to reach. Avoid an unfamiliar private residence, isolated route or a last-minute change to a secluded venue. If you have accessibility or transport requirements, raise them when planning.

Share a general meeting point with the other participant only once you have agreed to meet. You do not need to reveal your full home address, workplace, live location history or family details.

A simple, clear confirmation

“We agreed on a one-hour coffee conversation at the main entrance of a busy café at 4 pm. We will each arrange our own transport and pay for our own food unless we agree otherwise. If plans change, we'll message before leaving.”

This is a suggested example, not a promise that any specific meeting is safe.

3. Tell somebody you trust

  • Let a trusted friend or family member know the public venue, approximate timing and when you expect to return.
  • Keep your phone charged and bring enough money for your own journey home.
  • Use transport you can control; do not depend on a person you have just met for a ride.
  • Think ahead about how you will leave politely if the meeting feels uncomfortable.

These precautions are useful even when a profile looks polished. An account or profile photo does not automatically establish someone's identity, intentions or background.

4. Keep the rate and payment conversation clear

Before meeting, check the accepted hourly rate, expected duration, and which expenses are separate (tickets, food, transport or extra time). A listed profile rate is not necessarily the final rate if a different customer offer was accepted. Sathivo does not currently take booking payments or act as an escrow service: participants settle directly if they choose to pay.

Be cautious if someone insists on an unexpected advance payment, requests that you transfer money to a third party, or claims you must share a UPI PIN to receive money. Genuine transfers do not require revealing your PIN, OTP or banking password to another participant.

5. During the meeting: permission can change

Respect each other's personal space, time and comfort. Agree before changing the activity or extending the duration. A person who accepted a booking can still say no to a new request. Do not photograph, record or post about the other participant without the permissions required by law and their consent.

If the person pressures you to move somewhere private, demands secret payments, asks for sexual services, becomes threatening or will not accept your boundaries, end the interaction and leave. If you are in immediate danger in India, seek nearby help and contact emergency services; 112 is India's nationwide emergency number.

6. Online conversation needs boundaries too

Keep your login OTPs, passwords, government IDs, financial documents and account recovery details private. Consider what is visible behind you before sharing a photo. A photo marked for single viewing may still be copied by another camera or other method. You can stop chatting, block a user or report inappropriate conduct.

Everyday companionship is not medical advice, licensed counselling or crisis support. Seek a qualified professional for those needs.

7. After the meeting

Let your trusted contact know you returned safely. If something concerning happened, record the relevant facts and report it through the available in-app controls or Sathivo Support. Avoid posting someone else's private details in public to resolve a dispute.

If you want another booking, treat it as a new mutual decision. A friendly first interaction does not create an obligation to meet again.

Your one-minute checklist

  • Both people are 18+ and the plan is strictly platonic.
  • Activity, time, location and expected duration are mutually understood.
  • The hourly offer has been accepted; extra costs are clarified.
  • First in-person meetup is in a populated public place.
  • A trusted person knows the plan; I have my own return transport.
  • I know how to say no, leave, block or report.
