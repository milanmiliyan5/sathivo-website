Boundaries and mutual respect · Useful public guidance

Healthy boundaries before your first platonic meetup

A helpful way to agree on the activity, privacy, spending, time and respectful limits so that neither participant feels pushed into a plan they did not choose.

By Sathivo · Updated 21 November 2026 · Adults 18+ · Strictly platonic

Start with what you both want—not what you assume

“Boundaries” can sound formal, but in an ordinary social plan they are simply agreements about what is comfortable. Two adults might both enjoy a film yet have different expectations about conversation, photos, touch, spending, travel or how long to stay. A calm discussion before meeting prevents misunderstandings and gives both people a way to say no without a confrontation.

On Sathivo the relationship must remain strictly platonic. This means no romantic or sexual requests and no pressure to provide personal affection. A booking request is not permission to cross someone's boundaries. Acceptance of a plan is agreement to the described activity, not consent to every possible change.

1. Name the activity precisely

“Let's hang out” leaves many questions open. A clearer request might be “coffee and a conversation about books for one hour” or “company for a public art exhibition from 2 to 3:30 PM.” Describing the activity helps the companion decide whether they genuinely want to take part. If a listing only offers online conversation, do not assume an in-person invitation is welcome.

Try a concise sentence: “I am interested in a quiet café conversation. I prefer to stay at the venue, and I will arrange my own journey.” It is courteous, specific and does not require either person to reveal private information before they feel comfortable.

2. Agree on time and an easy ending

Pick a start time, approximate end time and a simple place to meet or connect online. “One hour” should mean one hour unless both people agree to change it. This is especially important when a companion has an hourly rate. If a long film, delayed bus or extra errand might extend the plan, discuss that possibility in advance. Nobody should feel obliged to stay longer because someone else bought a ticket.

An exit statement does not need a long explanation: “I will need to finish at 4 PM, so let's wrap up then.” If a meeting becomes uncomfortable, either person can end it earlier. Privacy, safety and mutual respect matter more than appearing polite at any cost.

3. Set limits for personal information

Before the meetup, share only details that are needed to coordinate the activity. A public entrance or well-known meeting point may be enough. You generally do not need to disclose a home address, government ID scan, family details, banking login or personal social-media account. Likewise, don't pressure the other person for a phone number simply because a booking was accepted.

Keep initial planning in Sathivo's booking chat where possible so the activity and agreed terms can be checked later. Be cautious when someone tries to move the discussion immediately to an unfamiliar link or asks for remote-screen access. Never give another member an OTP or UPI PIN.

4. Ask before taking photos or sharing details

A companion might welcome a photo of a museum display yet not want their own face photographed. Ask before taking or posting an image of another person. A picture shared privately should not become a public post without permission. Do not reveal screenshots of a private conversation or booking details to other people merely to tell a story online.

“Would you mind if I take a picture of the artwork? I won't include you” is more considerate than assuming permission. And a refusal is a complete answer: respond by putting the camera away rather than arguing.

5. Treat physical space and language respectfully

Platonic company should be free from unwanted touch, sexual remarks, repeated romantic suggestions or jokes that make someone uncomfortable. You do not have to wait until a situation escalates before expressing discomfort. “I would like to keep the conversation about the event” or “Please do not touch me” clearly describes the boundary.

Watch how you respond to another person's limit. A considerate answer is short: “Understood, I won't do that.” Asking repeatedly for an explanation or negotiating after a clear no can turn a simple disagreement into pressure.

6. Put money on the same page as expectations

Sathivo companions choose their listed rates and a customer may propose a different hourly rate. Before meeting, agree on the accepted offer, intended duration and whether food, travel, tickets or extra time are paid separately. Paying for an activity does not entitle anyone to romance, intimate contact or a change of boundaries.

Illustrative budget discussion

“We agreed on ₹300 per hour for a 90-minute exhibition visit. That is ₹450 for companion time. I'll buy my own ticket. Please confirm whether you will buy yours, and let's finish at the main exit at 5 PM.”

This is a mathematical example, not a real booking or a rate Sathivo guarantees. A person may decline it.

Payments between companions and customers are handled directly outside Sathivo. The optional Razorpay contribution supports the website itself; it is not a deposit for an individual. If a person is asking for unusual advance transfers or creating urgency, do not share credentials or pay without understanding the arrangement.

7. Handle a change or refusal without escalation

Plans change: a companion may prefer a different café, the weather may be bad, or a user may need to cancel. Suggest a change with room for a genuine no: “The outdoor park might be too hot. Would you prefer a public indoor café, or should we cancel?” If a proposal is rejected, respect the answer. No one owes an alternative date.

If somebody keeps insisting on a private venue, sexual activity, sharing a phone number or any other unwanted action, end the discussion. Sathivo offers report and block tools and a Support form. Reports are assessed, not automatically punished with a predetermined outcome.

8. A short boundary check before you go

  • Have you named an activity both people agreed to?
  • Do you know the start, finish and a public meeting point?
  • Do you each have independent travel or an exit plan?
  • Is the rate, duration and extra spending clear?
  • Have you kept sensitive information private?
  • Would you feel able to say no if plans changed?

You do not need perfect wording. Clarity, consent and respect are what make a social plan low-pressure. Read the first-meeting checklist for practical arrival and safety tips. For the binding platform rules, use the Safety and Community Guidelines and Terms of Use.
