Build a companion profile people can actually understand

A profile is useful when someone can tell what you offer, where you are available, how much you charge and what you will not do. Here is a step-by-step approach for Sathivo's strictly platonic marketplace.

By Sathivo · Updated 14 October 2026 · For adults 18+ · Strictly platonic

1. Explain the experience you offer

People browsing Sathivo are choosing an activity, not buying an unspecified promise of attention. Before you publish a profile, decide which everyday social experiences you would genuinely enjoy and can reliably provide: coffee conversations, visits to public places, movie outings, shopping company, walks, or online conversation. Select only the categories that match your intentions.

Sathivo is strictly platonic and for adults 18+. It does not permit dating arrangements, sexual requests, escort services or paid intimacy. Set that boundary clearly rather than leaving your description open to interpretation.

2. Write a short bio with specifics

Generic descriptions such as “I am available for anything” make it difficult for another person to plan. Instead, describe your interests, the kinds of conversations or outings you enjoy, languages you are comfortable using, and your preferred way of meeting.

Example bio for inspiration (not a live profile)

“I enjoy casual conversations about films, books and everyday life. I prefer daytime café meetings or walks in busy public places. I speak Odia, Hindi and English. I also enjoy online conversation when in-person plans are not possible. Strictly platonic plans only.”

Only use details that truthfully describe you. Do not copy this sample unchanged or claim languages, interests or availability you do not have.

Skip exaggerated promises such as “always available”, “guaranteed fun” or “verified identity” unless those statements are accurate and demonstrable. Sathivo does not currently provide a government-ID verification guarantee for every profile.

3. Set the right location and meeting preference

Choose your actual state, district and city or town. If your town is not among the suggestions, use the available custom city or town field. An accurate location helps people avoid sending requests you cannot reasonably accept. Do not list multiple distant cities just to appear in more searches.

Select whether you are open to online plans, in-person plans or the meeting options actually supported on your profile. For in-person meetings, you can discuss a public meeting area after a booking is accepted; do not put your exact home address in a public bio.

4. Choose an hourly price that makes sense to you

Companions—not Sathivo—set their hourly rate. Think about travel time, the type of social activity, the duration you are willing to commit to, and whether customers may have separate costs such as café orders or movie tickets. Put a real rate you would be comfortable accepting rather than an unrealistic price designed only to attract clicks.

How offers differ from your listed price

If you publish a rate of ₹400 per hour and someone requests ₹350 per hour, that lower amount is only an offer. You decide whether to accept it. Accepting records the offered hourly amount as the agreed rate. Sathivo does not collect this payment or negotiate it for you.

Never ask someone to pay a “Sathivo verification fee” or pretend the platform guarantees payment. Any direct payment arrangement needs clear mutual agreement.

5. Use a respectful image and protect your privacy

Choose a clear, appropriate profile image or the built-in avatar option where available. Use only a photo you have permission to share. Avoid identifiable documents, school or work badges, screenshots of other people's faces, personal phone numbers or images that misrepresent you. A photo may help recognition, but it is not proof of government-verified identity.

Do not publish your bank details, UPI PIN, full address or personal contact information in an attempt to get faster bookings. Keep booking coordination on the platform when possible.

6. Publish only when you can respond responsibly

Take a moment to review your activity choices, location, hourly rate and profile wording before publishing. Keep information up to date as your schedule changes. If you cannot make a proposed date or do not agree to the customer's offer, decline instead of accepting and disappearing.

When a booking is accepted, discuss the agreed activity, public venue, duration and boundaries in the participant chat. You can say no or end a plan if you feel uncomfortable or unsafe.

7. A quick self-check before making the profile public

  • Does the bio tell a visitor what conversation or activity I genuinely offer?
  • Are the town, meeting mode, language and activity choices accurate?
  • Does my listed hourly rate mean what I think it means?
  • Have I avoided private addresses, IDs, false verification claims and misleading photos?
  • Are my terms and boundaries respectful, legal and strictly platonic?
  • Would I understand this profile if I were the customer seeing it for the first time?

8. What happens after you publish

Your published profile may be discoverable in the Sathivo directory, depending on the applicable filters and current service state. Booking requests are decisions you can make individually; you are not required to accept every offer. Earnings are not guaranteed, and Sathivo currently does not process the money.
