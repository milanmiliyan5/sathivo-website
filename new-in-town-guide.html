Moving to a new city · Practical guidance

New in town? Make a simple, safer plan for exploring

Moving somewhere new can make everyday plans feel unfamiliar. These practical steps help adults explore public places with platonic company without overpromising local availability or treating an online profile as an identity check.

By Sathivo · Updated 21 November 2026 · 18+ · Strictly platonic

1. Start with the kind of outing you actually enjoy

In a new city, it can be tempting to look for someone who knows everything about the area. A better first step is to choose an activity that already interests you: a public exhibition, a café conversation, bookstore browsing, an afternoon market or a short walk in a populated park. When the activity itself is enjoyable, the plan does not depend on instant friendship or a stranger acting as a professional guide.

Sathivo is an adults-only platform for strictly platonic companionship. Published companions describe the activities they offer and set their own hourly rates. They are not automatically trained tour guides, translators or verified local experts. Read each listing's actual interests, languages and meeting preferences before making a request.

2. Use location filters—but do not assume there is a match

On Explore, begin with your state and district, then narrow the area or activity if there are suitable listings. If you see no results, try a nearby location that is genuinely practical to reach or consider an online conversation when a member has listed that option. Do not interpret a suggested city in a dropdown as evidence that a particular companion is available there.

A published profile can become unavailable or change its activities. Read the latest public description and wait for the recipient to accept a request. A profile photo or an email-verification indicator is not a background check, professional accreditation or proof that somebody will arrive at the meeting point.

3. Choose one public area rather than an ambitious itinerary

On a first outing, moving between several unfamiliar parts of a city creates unnecessary travel and timing problems. Choose one well-known public meeting point: the main entrance of a museum, an accessible café near a transit stop, a large public bookshop or a community event venue. Confirm its opening hours from the venue itself. Avoid agreeing to visit a private home, isolated location or unknown pickup spot with someone you have not met.

Think about the route back before making plans. Note which bus, metro, taxi or other transport is available to you, and set an end time that leaves you comfortable options to return independently. A busy central location is not a guarantee of safety, but it makes a first meeting easier to navigate.

4. Tell the other person what kind of company you want

“Show me the city” can sound broad. Instead, send a focused request: “I recently moved here and would enjoy a one-hour walk through the public museum exhibition with someone interested in art. I will arrange my own transport. Would you be comfortable with that activity?” This describes an interest without demanding the other person act as a paid city guide.

Ask about language preferences or accessibility needs when relevant. For example, “Would you prefer Hindi or English for our conversation?” is practical. Avoid asking for personal addresses, a government ID, employment details or family history before the plan.

5. Calculate the entire cost, not only the hourly offer

Suppose a companion lists ₹300 per hour and you propose a 90-minute public exhibition visit. At an accepted ₹300 hourly rate, the companion time would be ₹450. Tickets, snacks and your own transport are different costs. Agree who pays for each item before meeting, and check whether the exhibition is actually ticketed. Nothing in this example confirms that a real listing or event exists.

Sathivo currently does not collect, hold or automatically refund companion booking payments. Any lawful payment between the two participants is arranged directly outside the platform. The separate optional Razorpay platform contribution is not a companion deposit or fee for unlocking acceptance. The budget planner can help compare durations and spending.

6. Give your first meetup an uncomplicated ending

A 45- or 60-minute plan can be easier than a whole afternoon, particularly if you have never met. Agree on a start, approximate finish and a visible public exit. You can say, “Let's meet by the café entrance at 3 PM and finish at 4 PM.” Both people should be free to decline changes and leave when the agreed time ends.

Let a trusted person know the public venue and when you expect to return. Keep your phone charged, carry only what you need, and avoid sharing an OTP, UPI PIN, bank password or ID scan. Use Sathivo's first-meeting checklist for a fuller preparation routine.

7. If the city feels overwhelming, start online

An online conversation may be a lower-effort way to discuss local interests before meeting in person, but only if the published companion offers an online meeting mode. Decide whether text, voice or another mutually supported option is comfortable, and keep early coordination inside the platform's available chat. Nobody should pressure you to turn on a camera or share personal messaging accounts.

For an online conversation, the same platonic rules apply. You may talk about favourite public events, food traditions, literature or local transport experiences without revealing where you live. Online familiarity is not identity verification, so an in-person meeting still deserves independent planning.

8. Practise polite refusals before you need one

A new acquaintance might suggest a different place, a longer booking or a personal favour. You are allowed to say, “I'd prefer the public venue we agreed on,” or “I need to finish on time; let's leave it there.” If the other person declines your suggestion, respect that too. Payment does not create an entitlement to private information, romantic behaviour or additional time.

If you encounter harassment, sexual requests, impersonation or pressure for suspicious transfers, do not continue simply to avoid awkwardness. Use the available report/block features or contact Support. Reports are reviewed; submitting one does not automatically prove misconduct or ensure an immediate account action.

9. A realistic first-day plan

Illustrative Saturday museum plan

Before booking: independently confirm a public museum's hours, entrance and ticket prices. In the request: propose a 60-minute visit, the meeting preference and an hourly offer. After acceptance: use booking chat to confirm the exact entrance, budget, transport and boundaries. On the day: tell a trusted person where you are going and leave at the agreed time.

This is a planning exercise, not a verified venue, member review or available booking. If no appropriate public listing exists in your area, don't force an unsuitable match.

10. Quick questions

Does Sathivo guarantee a local companion?

No. Availability depends on people who publish suitable profiles and choose to accept a request.

Can I ask someone to become my tourist guide?

You can describe a lawful public activity, but do not assume a companion provides regulated travel services or professional guiding. Ask what the person actually offers.

Is my first meeting required to become a friendship?

No. Platonic company can be a one-time agreed activity. Both participants retain their own preferences and boundaries.
